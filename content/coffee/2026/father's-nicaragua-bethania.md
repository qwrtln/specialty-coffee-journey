While hunting for [The Fermentation Project](https://www.thefermentationproject.com/) tasting kit, I noticed that Father's, a roaster I tried a [long](/coffee/2024/fathers-uganda-zebigi/) [time](/coffee/2020/fathers-ethiopia-bensa-asefa/) ago, offered it.
I added this coffee to the order as well.

Because I was quite well stocked, I brewed about 2/3 of the bag, and then didn't touch it for about a month.
Then, after returning to it, I observed a phenomenon that happens occasionally, but I can't explain it: 2-6 weeks after roasting, the coffee tastes okay-ish.
But after letting it sit until about 10-12 weeks after roasting, the taste becomes phenomenal.
White tea notes (although with a relatively heavy body) became very prominent in the last batch.
I was actually sad that there were so few brews left before I ran out.

This phenomenon of the taste improving dramatically as you enter the 10-12 week window needs some research.
Arguably, it's way past the standard time it takes for most of the CO₂ to escape.
However, according to [Scott Rao](https://www.scottrao.com/blog/restingbeans), there's no rule of thumb.
